Part A · Plan

Before you buy a single screw: know what a server is, what yours must do, and where to get it.

01Plan

What is a server, really?

An ordinary computer, rearranged to serve many people, around the clock, with nobody sitting at it.

Start with a desktop

Same ingredients you already know: a CPU, memory, drives, a power supply and fans.

Lay it down, make it thin

Servers stack in racks, so they're flat and wide. Height is measured in “U” — one U is 1.75 inches.

Air goes one way

Fans line up into a fan wall. Cool air enters the front, hot air leaves the back. No exceptions.

Everything doubled, everything swappable

Two power supplies and front drive bays you can pull while it runs. One part dies, the server keeps serving.

Into the rack

Rack ears, rails, and a tiny remote-control computer inside. It will run for years without a monitor or keyboard.

Its job: answer requests

A server is a computer whose job is answering requests from other computers — websites, files, games. It runs all day, every day, usually with no screen attached.

Built for many, not one

Your laptop is built for one person. A server is built for many people at once, and keeps running even when a part fails.

Familiar parts, plus extras

Servers add error-correcting memory, spare power supplies and a built-in remote-control chip.

02Plan

Start with the job, not the parts

Decide what the server will do. The job picks the parts — and finds your bottleneck.

Never shop first

Decide the job — store files, host a game, run virtual machines — and let that job pick the parts.

Every job is hungry for something

File servers crave disk space. Databases crave fast storage and memory. AI craves GPU memory.

Plan two years ahead

A little extra capacity now costs far less than replacing the whole server later.

03Plan

Buy, build, or refurb?

You're trading money for support for control. The price tag is only part of the cost.

Brand-name

Dell PowerEdge, HPE ProLiant, Lenovo ThinkSystem: tested, warrantied, phone support. You pay for peace of mind — and sometimes feature licenses.

Barebones

Supermicro, ASRock Rack, Gigabyte: case, board and power supplies included. You add CPU, memory and drives.

Refurbished

Off-lease business servers are shockingly cheap, but older, louder and hungry for electricity. Check the power bill first.

Full DIY

Total freedom — and you become the warranty department when something doesn't work.

Part B · Hardware

Every component, what its numbers mean, and how those numbers change real performance.

04Hardware · Chassis

Shapes & sizes: towers, racks and “U”

U is just height. Thin means dense but loud. Taller means more room and quieter fans.

Measured in U

One U is 1.75 in (44.45 mm). A “2U server” is 3.5 inches tall and slides into a standard 19-inch rack.

Thin boxes scream

1U servers need tiny, fast 40 mm fans. Taller 2U and 4U boxes use bigger, slower fans and fit more drives.

No rack? Tower.

A tower server stands on the floor like a desktop, runs quieter, and is perfect for an office closet.

Measure depth!

Many rack servers are 28–32 in deep and won't fit a shallow wall-mounted rack.

05Hardware · Motherboard

The motherboard and PCIe lanes

The board is the city map. PCIe lanes are highways — a budget you spend.

The city map

Every part plugs into the motherboard, and it decides how many lanes of traffic each part gets.

Lanes are highways

A GPU wants 16 lanes; an NVMe drive wants 4. Run out of lanes and parts slow down or don't work.

Each generation doubles

A PCIe 5.0 drive on 4 lanes moves about 16 GB/s — a 4K movie in seconds.

The socket must match

An AMD EPYC chip will never fit an Intel Xeon board, and vice versa. Check BIOS support for newer CPUs too.

Numbers to know · PCIe
GenerationPer lane (one way)x4 (NVMe)x16 (GPU)
Gen 3≈ 1 GB/s≈ 4 GB/s≈ 16 GB/s
Gen 4≈ 2 GB/s≈ 8 GB/s≈ 32 GB/s

CPU lane budgets: AMD EPYC 9005 = 128 lanes · Intel Xeon 6700P = 88 · entry chips ≈ 16–28.

Common mistakes

  • Physical x16 slot wired x8
  • M.2 sharing lanes with SATA
  • Forgetting bifurcation
  • Old BIOS, new CPU
  • Risers sold separately

06Hardware · CPU

The CPU: workers and speed

Cores are how many jobs at once. Clock is how fast each job goes. Your workload decides which matters.

Cores = workers

Lots of users? Get more workers. One heavy task? Get faster workers (higher GHz).

Threads ≠ double

Threads let each core juggle two tasks. That adds roughly 10–30% more throughput, not 2×.

Cache = a desk nearby

A bigger cache means fewer slow trips down the hall to the memory “freezer”.

TDP = heat

Roughly how many watts of heat the chip makes: 65–500 W on servers. Cooling and power must keep up.

Common mistakes

  • Old dual Xeons idling at 150 W+
  • Many slow cores for game servers
  • Forgetting per-core licensing
  • Wrong heatsink height (1U vs 2U)

07Hardware · Memory

Memory (RAM): the workbench

RAM is the desk — fast and small. Storage is the filing cabinet — big and slow. Servers use ECC because silent mistakes are the scariest kind.

The workbench

If the job doesn't fit on the bench, the server shuffles work to slow storage — and everything crawls.

ECC isn't optional

Error-correcting memory catches and fixes flipped bits before they corrupt your data.

Fill every channel

Memory travels on channels, like checkout lanes. 12 sticks across 12 channels beats 6 sticks with half the lanes closed.

Use identical sticks

Mixing sizes, speeds or types can slow everything down, or stop the server from booting.

  • Unbalanced channels
  • Mixing RDIMM & LRDIMM
  • Consumer non-ECC RAM
  • Paying for speed over capacity

08Hardware · Storage

Storage: trucks vs bikes

Spinning disks are cheap capacity but slow at random work. NVMe is a highway straight to the CPU.

Trucks and bikes

Throughput is how much moves at once — a truck. IOPS is how many small trips per second — a swarm of bikes. Databases need bikes; video needs trucks.

200 vs 1,000,000

A hard drive manages about 200 random reads per second. An NVMe SSD can do over a million.

HDDs still win on price

About 30 TB per drive for cheap — perfect for backups and media libraries.

SSDs wear out

“1 DWPD” means you can rewrite the whole drive once a day, every day, for the warranty.

Numbers to know · drives
TypeSequentialRandom IOPSLatencyBest for
HDD 7,200 rpm≈ 250 MB/s≈ 2005–10 msBulk, backup, media

Common mistakes

  • SMR drives in RAID
  • Consumer SSDs without power-loss protection
  • NVMe in SATA-only bays
  • Booting from the data array

09Hardware · Data protection

RAID: surviving a dead drive

RAID keeps you running when a drive dies. It does not save you from mistakes.

Many drives, one volume

Depending on the level you gain speed, safety, or both — but always trade away some capacity.

Pick a level

RAID 1 mirrors. RAID 10 mirrors and stripes: fast and safe. RAID 6 survives any two drives dying.

Rebuilds are fragile

Rebuilding a huge drive can take a day or more, and the array is vulnerable until it finishes.

RAID is not a backup

Delete a file or get hit by ransomware and RAID faithfully destroys every copy instantly.

Common mistakes

  • No spare drive on the shelf
  • Ignoring “degraded” alerts
  • Thinking RAID is a backup

10Hardware · Power

Power: watts in

Two power supplies on two circuits means pulling one changes nothing — if one alone can carry the whole load.

Redundant supplies

Two hot-swap PSUs share the load. If one dies, the other carries everything — so each must be big enough alone.

Size with headroom

Add up every part's maximum watts, then add 25%. A 730 W build → 2 × 1100 W supplies.

Efficiency = less heat

80 PLUS Titanium wastes about 4% as heat; Bronze wastes 15%. Waste costs money twice: power and cooling.

A UPS is a battery bridge

It rides through power blips and gives the server minutes to shut down cleanly in an outage.

Common mistakes

  • Sizing by both PSUs combined
  • Both PSUs on one power strip
  • Forgetting 120 V derating
  • No UPS auto-shutdown

11Hardware · Cooling

Cooling: heat out

Every watt in becomes heat out. Air must flow one way through a sealed path — every gap or dead fan makes a hot spot.

Front to back

Servers breathe cool air in the front and blow hot air out the back. Never block either end.

Shrouds and blanks matter

Missing the plastic air shroud or a blanking panel? Air sneaks around the parts instead of through them.

Fans are N+1

A fan wall can lose one fan; the others ramp up. Passive heatsinks rely entirely on that wall.

1 W = 3.41 BTU/hr

A 300 W server heats a room like a small space heater on low. Plan the exhaust.

Common mistakes

  • Running without the shroud
  • Closed closet, no exhaust
  • Server mounted backwards
  • Passive GPU in a quiet tower

12Hardware · Networking

Networking hardware: the pipe to the world

Bandwidth is pipe width. Ports and cables must match. And the slowest link always wins.

Bits ÷ 8 = bytes

1 GbE moves about 115 MB/s — slower than one modern hard drive.

10 GbE is the sweet spot

Copying a 50 GB folder drops from about 7 minutes to under 1.

RJ45 or SFP

RJ45 is the familiar Ethernet jack. SFP is a cage for copper DAC cables or fiber modules. Your switch must match.

Slowest link wins

Drive, cable, switch, or the laptop on the other end — the narrowest pipe sets your real speed.

13Hardware · Remote management

The BMC: a computer inside your computer

A tiny always-on computer that lets you fix your server from anywhere — even when it's “off”.

Always awake

The BMC keeps working while the server is off, as long as it's plugged in.

Remote hands

From a browser: power on, watch the screen, install an OS, read temperatures — without touching the server.

Same idea, different badge

Dell calls it iDRAC. HPE calls it iLO. Lenovo: XClarity. Supermicro: BMC/IPMI.

A master key

Change its default password and never expose it to the internet. Keep it on its own management network.

Common mistakes

  • BMC on the public internet
  • Default password left on
  • Never updating BMC firmware
  • Missing license for remote console

14Hardware · Accelerators OPTIONAL

GPUs and accelerators

GPUs do huge amounts of simple math in parallel. Most servers don't need one — AI, video and 3D do.

When you need one

AI, video transcoding, 3D rendering: jobs made of thousands of small math problems at once.

VRAM decides what fits

At 4-bit, plan about 0.5–0.6 GB per billion AI model parameters, plus extra.

Passive cards need wind

Server GPUs often have no fans. Put one in a quiet tower and it cooks.

Power & fit

Big GPUs draw 300–600 W and need special cables and risers. Confirm your server supports the card.

Part C · Build

Put it together carefully, in the right order — every connector only fits one way for a reason.

15Build · Assembly

Putting it together

Building is a sequence of small, careful, keyed steps. Inside out, then bench-test before racking.

Ground yourself

Static you can't feel can kill a chip. Wear a wrist strap clipped to bare chassis metal.

Inside out

CPU and memory first, then drives and cards, then cables — so nothing blocks the next step.

Bench test first

Finding a dead memory stick is easy on a table and miserable in a rack.

Two people, bottom up

A loaded server can weigh 30 kg. Lift with two people and fill racks from the bottom.

Part D · Software

Wake the hardware up, give it an operating system, an address, locks and a safety net.

16Software · Firmware

First power-on: firmware, BIOS, storage

Firmware is the server's pre-flight checklist. Boot is slow because it checks everything.

Be patient

First boot can take several minutes while memory is “trained”. Black screen + roaring fans is normal.

Update firmware first

Order: BMC → BIOS → storage card → network card → drives. Fixes bugs and closes security holes.

Few BIOS settings

Turn on virtualization, UEFI boot and Secure Boot; pick a power profile. Leave the rest at defaults.

Set up storage now

Create a RAID volume in the controller — or switch it to HBA mode if ZFS will manage the drives.

17Software · Operating system

Installing the operating system

Pick the OS by job. A hypervisor turns one server into many.

Pick by job

Ubuntu Server for general apps, Proxmox VE for virtual machines, TrueNAS for file storage, Windows Server for Microsoft shops.

No monitor needed

Mount the installer through the BMC's virtual media and click through setup from your laptop.

Separate boot drives

Install the OS on small mirrored boot drives. Reinstalling then never touches your data.

Licensing matters

Windows Server is licensed per CPU core, 16-core minimum. More cores = bigger bill.

18Software · Network & security

Connect and lock it down

An address, a street, an exit and a phone book. Then: keys beat passwords, close everything you don't need, deny by default.

A fixed address

A static IP (or a reservation on your router) means you can always find the server — like a house number.

SSH keys, not passwords

The private key stays on your laptop; the public “padlock” goes on the server.

Block everything first

Firewall rule one: deny all incoming, then open only what you need.

Auto-update

Most break-ins use bugs patched months earlier. Turn on automatic security updates. Use a VPN instead of port-forwarding.

19Software · Operations

Watch it and back it up

Three copies, two kinds of media, one offsite — and actually test restores. Different disasters destroy different copies.

Alerts before outages

Alert on disk health, temperatures and full drives. A 2 a.m. email beats a dead array on Monday.

3-2-1

3 copies, on 2 kinds of storage, with 1 somewhere else.

Test restores

A backup you've never restored is a hope, not a backup.

One copy offline

Keep one copy offline or immutable, so ransomware that reaches your server can't reach it.

Part E · Go live

Serve your first request — then design a whole server for your own workload.

20Go live

Your first service — and your own build

A balanced server has no single narrow pipe. Buy for your workload, not for the biggest numbers.

Payoff time

Install a web server or file share, open its port in the firewall, then visit the server's address from another device.

Containers

Package an app with everything it needs, so dozens of services run side by side without fighting.

Write it all down

Addresses, what's installed, where backups go. Future you will forget — and be very grateful.
